Capsule size guide: choosing between size 00 and size 4

Multicaps Technical Team2 November 20266 min read

Picking the right capsule size is one of the first decisions in developing a capsule product. Too small and your dose will not fit; too large and you waste shell material, risk poor fill uniformity and make the product harder to swallow. This guide explains how capsule sizes work and how to choose one with confidence.

How capsule sizes are numbered

Hard gelatin capsules follow a standard numbering system in which a larger number means a smaller capsule. Size 4 is the smallest in common pharmaceutical use, and size 00 is the largest of the standard range. Between them sit sizes 3, 2, 1 and 0.

There is also size 0el ("elongated"), which has the same diameter as size 0 but a longer body. It adds roughly 15% more capacity without making the capsule any wider, which helps when a formulation is just slightly too large for a size 0.

How to calculate the right size

The key figure is the volume your fill occupies, which depends on both its weight and its density. The basic calculation is:

Required capsule volume (ml) = fill weight (g) ÷ tapped bulk density (g/ml)

For example, a 400 mg dose of a powder with a tapped density of 0.6 g/ml needs 0.4 ÷ 0.6 = 0.67 ml. That sits right at the capacity of a size 0 capsule (0.68 ml), so in practice you would trial size 0 and consider 0el for a safety margin.

Remember that filling machines rarely achieve tapped density exactly. Dosator and tamping-pin machines compress the powder to differing degrees, so always confirm your choice with a filling trial using real samples.

Other factors to consider

Patient swallowability

Sizes 0 and 1 are the most widely used for adult medicines. Size 00 suits high-dose products and nutraceuticals, while sizes 3 and 4 are better for low-dose actives and for patients who struggle with larger capsules.

Machine change parts

Each capsule size needs matching change parts on your filling machine. Standardising on a few sizes across your portfolio reduces changeover time and tooling costs.

Excipients and flow

Adding a glidant or filler changes both weight and density. Fix your formulation before finalising the capsule size, and allow some headroom for scale-up.

Branding

Larger capsules give more space for printing and make colour combinations more visible, which helps patients recognise their medicine.

Getting it right first time

If you are unsure which size fits your formulation, our technical team can recommend options based on your fill weight and powder characteristics, and send samples for a filling trial.

Quick checklist
  • Know your fill weight per capsule and the tapped density of the blend
  • Calculate the required volume and pick the smallest size with some headroom
  • Consider 0el before stepping up from 0 to 00
  • Confirm with a filling trial on your own machine
