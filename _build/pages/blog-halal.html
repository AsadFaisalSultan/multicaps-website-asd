What makes a gelatin capsule halal?

Multicaps Technical Team2 November 20265 min read

For pharmaceutical companies serving Muslim patients, in Pakistan and in export markets across the Middle East and South-East Asia, the halal status of every ingredient matters. The capsule shell is often overlooked, yet it is usually the largest single excipient in a capsule product. Here is what makes a gelatin capsule halal, and how to verify it.

Where gelatin comes from

Gelatin is a protein obtained from collagen found in animal skin, bones and connective tissue. Pharmaceutical gelatin is typically made from bovine (cattle) or fish sources, and in some markets from porcine sources. The source animal, and how it was slaughtered, determine whether the gelatin can be considered halal.

For gelatin to be halal it must come from a permissible source: from animals slaughtered according to Islamic requirements, or from fish. Porcine-derived gelatin is never acceptable, and neither is any contact with it during processing.

It is not only the gelatin

A halal capsule requires every component and every step to comply, not just the main raw material:

  • Colours and inks: colourants, opacifiers and printing inks must be free from prohibited ingredients.
  • Processing aids: lubricants, plasticisers and any other aids used in manufacture must also be halal.
  • Segregation: production lines and storage must prevent cross-contamination with non-halal materials.
  • Traceability: every lot of gelatin must be traceable back to a certified supplier.

The role of certification

Because buyers cannot verify any of this by testing the finished capsule alone, halal status rests on certification. A recognised halal certification body audits the manufacturer's sourcing, processes, documentation and segregation controls, and re-audits periodically to keep the certificate valid.

Documents to request from your supplier

When qualifying a capsule supplier, ask for:

  1. A current halal certificate issued to the capsule manufacturer, with its validity dates and scope.
  2. Halal certificates for the gelatin suppliers they use.
  3. A TSE/BSE-free statement or certificate for the gelatin.
  4. A certificate of analysis for each batch you receive.

Check that certificates are current and that the scope covers the products you are buying.

Why it matters

Using certified halal capsules protects patient trust, simplifies registration in markets that require halal compliance, and removes a common barrier to export. For a product sold to millions of patients, it is a small decision with a large impact.
