Common challenges in capsule filling, and how quality capsules prevent them

Multicaps Technical Team21 April 20258 min read

Capsule filling is one of the most critical steps in manufacturing oral solid dosage forms. Whether you run semi-automatic or fully automatic filling machines, the consistency and quality of the finished product depend heavily on the physical and mechanical properties of the empty hard gelatin capsules you feed in.

Modern equipment is highly advanced, yet manufacturers still face recurring challenges on the filling line. Many of them can be reduced or eliminated by using high-quality capsules with consistent specifications, which improves efficiency, lowers rejection rates and supports regulatory compliance.

1. Capsule separation failure

Filling begins by separating the cap from the body. If the halves are joined too tightly, deformed or inconsistently pre-locked, the machine can fail to separate them, causing downtime, misfeeds and product loss. Common causes are high moisture content, poor dimensional uniformity and incorrect pre-lock torque.

How quality capsules help: tight dimensional tolerances and moisture held in the optimal range of roughly 13–16% give consistent wall thickness and dome curvature, allowing smooth separation even at high speeds.

2. Fill weight variation

Inconsistent fill weights cause content uniformity problems that can lead to non-compliance or recalls. Variation in body length or internal diameter, poor powder flow and electrostatic attraction between powder and capsule wall all contribute.

How quality capsules help: precise internal volumes with minimal variability, combined with smooth, low-static surfaces, improve powder flow and dosing accuracy.

3. Locking issues after filling

After filling, the cap must lock securely onto the body to prevent leakage or tampering. Weak locking lets capsules open during handling, packing or transport. The usual causes are an inconsistent cap-body fit, poor groove geometry or deformation during filling.

How quality capsules help: robust locking features, such as a snap-fit groove, give strong and uniform engagement that keeps capsules closed throughout their life.

4. Breakage or cracking

Brittle or poorly plasticised capsules can crack during feeding or closing, leading to rejected units and machine stoppages. Typical causes include low moisture content (below about 12%), uneven plasticiser distribution in the gelatin film and insufficient wall thickness.

How quality capsules help: controlled moisture during drying keeps shells flexible, while good manufacturing practice ensures even plasticiser distribution and consistent wall thickness for better structural integrity.

5. Static and dust build-up

Static charge makes shells cling to machine parts and dosator pins, causing dosing errors. Low humidity, surface charge build-up and poor grounding make it worse.

How quality capsules help: attention to surface properties reduces static, and smooth interiors prevent powder adhesion, keeping lines cleaner and dosing more precise.

6. Incompatibility with the fill

Some APIs and excipients interact with the capsule shell, particularly hygroscopic or reactive compounds. Water migration from shell to fill, strongly acidic or alkaline APIs and reactive excipients can all cause problems.

How quality capsules help: modified formulations, such as low-moisture capsules, are available for moisture-sensitive fills. Talk to your supplier early in development.

7. Machine compatibility and downtime

Jams and downtime often happen when capsules lack dimensional consistency or mechanical strength and collapse under machine pressure.

How quality capsules help: camera and laser inspection maintain tight dimensional tolerances, and testing for crush strength, roundness and weight uniformity ensures compatibility with high-speed machines.

Conclusion

While filling equipment continues to evolve, consistent capsule quality remains the most influential factor in efficient production. Separation failure, locking problems, leakage and fill weight variation can be significantly reduced by sourcing capsules that meet strict pharmaceutical-grade standards.

Investing in quality capsules pays back in:
  • Reduced downtime and maintenance
  • Higher batch yield
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Better final product quality
